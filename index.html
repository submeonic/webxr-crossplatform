Interactive WebXR companion to LearnQM Chapter 1

Hydrogen Orbitals in 3D

This experience reinterprets the orbital simulations from LearnQM’s semiconductor-physics chapter as interactive three-dimensional probability clouds. Explore on a desktop or touch screen, or enter WebXR to stand beside the atom and inspect how repeated electron measurements fill space around the nucleus.

Interaction Tutorial

Explore the Chapter Spatially

Each point in the viewer represents one possible result of measuring an electron’s position. Orbit around the cloud to see that its structure exists in every direction, then move the thin spherical shell through the measurements to connect the 3D shape with the radial graph below the lesson text.

On desktop, drag sideways to orbit, drag vertically to move the shell, and scroll to zoom. On touch devices, use the same one-finger drags and pinch to zoom. In WebXR, pinch and move vertically to adjust the shell while the world-space guide shows the direction and sensitivity of the control.

Simulation 01

The 1s Orbital

The 1s orbital is hydrogen’s lowest-energy state. The pale yellow points are not particles moving along fixed paths; together they show the probability pattern produced by repeating the same position measurement thousands of times.

Rotate the cloud to confirm that the distribution is spherical. Then drag the shell outward. Points inside the selected radial band brighten, and the graph reports how many measurements fall at that distance. The peak near one Bohr radius shows the most likely distance from the nucleus.

Simulation 02

The 2s Orbital

In the excited 2s state, the same number of measurements spreads across a much larger volume. The 3D cloud contains a small inner probability region and a broader outer region, separated by a spherical radial node near two Bohr radii where the probability falls to zero.

Orbit around the atom to see that the node is an empty spherical boundary rather than a flat gap. Move the shell through the inner region, across the node, and into the outer peak. The green 2s bars share the same axes and scale as the faint yellow 1s comparison, making the change in size and probability directly visible.

Simulation 03

The 2p Orbital

The 2p orbitals occupy the same principal energy level as 2s, but their probability is directional instead of spherical. A nodal plane passes through the nucleus and separates two lobes of likely electron measurements.

This section will use direct manipulation of the simulation object rather than idle motion, allowing the lobe orientation and nodal plane to be inspected deliberately in three dimensions.
