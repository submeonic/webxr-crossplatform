LearnQM · Chapter 1.3D

Hydrogen Orbitals in 3D

Where might you find hydrogen’s electron? In Chapter 1 you collected position measurements. Here, each dot marks one possible result in three dimensions. Turn the cloud to see which patterns survive a change of viewpoint.

Interaction Tutorial

Read a Probability Cloud

Closely spaced dots mark places where measurements are more likely in the same small volume. Choose Resample to repeat the experiment with a fresh set of dots.

Drag sideways to turn the cloud and vertically to move the measuring surface. You can also drag across the graph or focus it and use the arrow keys. Zoom with the scroll wheel or a two-finger pinch.

Walk to change your viewpoint, or make a fist and steer with your thumb. Pinch and move up or down to adjust the measuring surface, or sideways to rotate the cloud. Look down to your left for the panel. It stays put when you stop. Press with either index finger and lift it away before pressing again. The eye dims or restores the panel.

Simulation 01

The 1s Orbital

Turn the 1s cloud. It looks much the same from every side, with dots packed closest near the nucleus. Slide the thin shell outward and watch the highlighted dots. Each shell has the same thickness, but a larger shell has more room. The graph peaks near one Bohr radius (a0), where that shell is most likely to catch a measurement.

Simulation 02

The 2s Orbital

Scan from the small inner cloud into the larger outer cloud. Near 2a0 the graph drops to zero. That distance marks a radial node, a spherical surface with zero probability density. Turn on 90% Region to see how one sphere can enclose most of the probability and still contain that gap.

Simulation 03

The 2p Orbitals

Two points equally far from the nucleus can now have very different densities. Turn a 2p cloud to find its two lobes and the plane of zero density between them. A shell count combines all directions and hides that difference.

Choose a percentage to enclose the densest parts of both lobes together. Read the model probability across the graph and the required volume up it, in cubic Bohr radii (a0³). Yellow dots show the fraction of sampled outcomes inside each volume.

Simulation 04

The 3d Orbitals

Select 3dxy and turn its four lobes past the labeled axes. Then try 3dz². Two lobes point along Z and a ring wraps around the middle. Grow the enclosure to see which parts of the cloud enter first. The viewer scales these larger orbitals down to fit. Use graph volumes to compare their sizes.
